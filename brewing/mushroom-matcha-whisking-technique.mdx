## Why technique matters more with matcha than most tea

Unlike steeped tea, matcha (mushroom-blended or not) is a suspension, fine powder distributed through water rather than dissolved. How it's mixed directly affects texture and whether the powder stays evenly suspended or settles and clumps.

## The zigzag motion

Move the whisk back and forth in a rapid "W" or zigzag pattern, rather than stirring in circles, for roughly 15-20 seconds. This incorporates more air, producing the light foam associated with a well-whisked cup, and breaks up clumps more effectively than circular motion.

## Common mistakes

- **Stirring in circles** instead of zigzagging, leaving the powder under-incorporated
- **Not sifting clumpy powder first**, leading to visible lumps that whisking alone won't fully break up
- **Using too little water for the amount of powder**, making it hard to whisk smoothly regardless of technique

## What if you don't have a bamboo whisk

A small electric milk frother is a reasonable substitute and, for some people, easier to use consistently than a traditional chasen. Vigorous manual stirring with a spoon can work but generally produces a less smooth result.
