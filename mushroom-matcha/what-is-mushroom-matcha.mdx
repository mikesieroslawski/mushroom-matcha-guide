## The short answer

Mushroom matcha is ceremonial-grade Japanese matcha blended with Lion's Mane, Cordyceps, and Reishi mushroom powder into a single powder. It's not plain matcha with mushrooms added as an afterthought, and it's not a mushroom coffee with tea swapped in; it's formulated as one blend.

## What's actually in it

- **Ceremonial-grade matcha.** Shade-grown, stone-milled Japanese green tea, giving the blend its grassy, umami flavor and its real, meaningful caffeine content (commonly cited 30-70mg per cup, well above a low-caffeine drink).
- **Lion's Mane mushroom powder.** Whole mushroom powder, traditionally used in East Asian cooking and herbal practice, studied in a limited number of human trials for cognitive function.
- **Cordyceps mushroom powder.** Whole mushroom powder, traditionally associated with stamina and steady energy.
- **Reishi mushroom powder.** Whole mushroom powder, traditionally used for relaxation and general wellness.

## Why blend mushrooms into matcha instead of coffee

Matcha already carries real caffeine and a distinct amino-acid profile (L-theanine) that's associated with steadier alertness. Blending mushroom powder into matcha rather than coffee combines two ingredients already studied, separately, for complementary effects on focus and calm.

## How it's meant to be used

Formulated as a daily drink, not an occasional supplement. A typical bag is specified for around 28 servings at roughly 1g of powder per 8oz cup, whisked directly into warm water (140-175°F). See [How to Make Mushroom Matcha](/brewing/how-to-make-mushroom-matcha) for the full method.
