## Tea sourcing: what transparency looks like

A transparent product names the specific growing region (for example, Shizuoka Prefecture, Japan's largest tea-growing region), the cultivar or cultivar blend used (such as Saemidori or Tsuyuhikari), and the grade (ceremonial, from first-harvest, shade-grown leaf). "Product of Japan" alone, with no further detail, is a much lower transparency bar.

## Mushroom sourcing: what to look for

- **Cultivated vs wild-harvested.** Most commercial mushroom powder, including Lion's Mane, Cordyceps, and Reishi, comes from cultivated sources today, generally more consistent and sustainable than wild-harvesting.
- **Fruiting body vs mycelium.** Some products use the mushroom's fruiting body (the visible, harvestable part), others use mycelium (the root-like network) grown on a grain substrate. These aren't nutritionally identical, and a transparent label states which is used.
- **Country of origin disclosed** for the mushroom powder specifically, not just the tea.

## Why this level of detail matters

Sourcing claims are easy to make vaguely and hard to verify without specifics. A product willing to name its tea garden, cultivar, and mushroom form is giving a buyer more to actually check than one that doesn't.
