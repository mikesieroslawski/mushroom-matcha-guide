## The base drink decides the flavor category

Mushroom matcha tastes like matcha: grassy, umami-forward, mildly sweet when brewed correctly. Mushroom coffee tastes like coffee: roasted, often bitter or acidic, sometimes slightly muted compared to regular coffee depending on how much mushroom powder is included. These are fundamentally different flavor categories, not variations on the same taste.

## Why the mushroom powder doesn't bridge the gap

Both products use whole mushroom powder (commonly Lion's Mane, Cordyceps, Reishi in mushroom matcha; often Lion's Mane, Chaga, Cordyceps in mushroom coffee) in small enough quantities relative to the base drink that the mushroom flavor itself is subtle in both, an earthy undertone rather than a dominant taste. The base tea or coffee flavor is what actually differs.

## Which one tastes more like "regular" tea or coffee

Mushroom coffee generally reads as closer to a normal cup of coffee than mushroom matcha reads as a normal cup of tea, since matcha's flavor is already a bigger departure from what most people expect a "tea" to taste like (steeped black or green tea) than coffee is from itself.
