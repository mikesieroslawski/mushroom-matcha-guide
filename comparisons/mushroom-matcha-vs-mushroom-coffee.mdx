## The base drink is the real difference

| | Mushroom Coffee | Mushroom Matcha |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Roasted coffee | Ceremonial-grade Japanese matcha |
| Caffeine (per cup) | Commonly cited ~80-100mg, sometimes reduced with a decaf base | Commonly cited ~30-70mg |
| Flavor | Coffee-forward, often bitter or acidic | Grassy, umami, matcha-forward |
| Typical mushrooms used | Often Lion's Mane, Chaga, Cordyceps | Often Lion's Mane, Cordyceps, Reishi |

## Neither one is "low caffeine"

A common misconception is that any mushroom-blended drink is automatically low-caffeine. Mushroom coffee generally isn't (unless built on a decaf base), and mushroom matcha genuinely isn't either, both deliver real, meaningful caffeine, just from different base drinks with somewhat different totals.

## Why the mushrooms aren't the deciding factor

The mushroom powders used, Lion's Mane, Cordyceps, Reishi, and others, are typically included in broadly comparable amounts across both categories and contribute negligible caffeine either way. The caffeine and flavor differences between mushroom coffee and mushroom matcha come almost entirely from the base drink.

## Which one to pick

If the goal is keeping coffee's flavor and ritual while adding mushroom powder, mushroom coffee is the more direct fit. If the goal is matcha's grassy flavor and the caffeine-plus-L-theanine combination, mushroom matcha is the closer match.
