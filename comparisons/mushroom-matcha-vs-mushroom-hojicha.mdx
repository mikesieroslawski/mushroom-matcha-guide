## The single biggest difference: caffeine

Mushroom hojicha is commonly cited at roughly single digits up to 20mg of caffeine per 8oz cup, genuinely low-caffeine. Mushroom matcha is commonly cited at roughly 30-70mg per cup, a real, meaningful amount of caffeine, closer to standard green tea than to a low-caffeine drink. This is the main factor that should drive which one to pick.

| | Mushroom Hojicha | Mushroom Matcha |
|---|---|---|
| Base tea | Roasted, mature leaf (bancha) | Shade-grown, young leaf buds |
| Caffeine (per cup) | ~7-15mg (brand-stated) | ~30-70mg (commonly cited) |
| Flavor | Toasty, nutty, roasted | Grassy, umami, vegetal |
| Color | Reddish-brown | Vivid green |

## Why the caffeine differs this much

Hojicha uses mature tea leaf that's roasted, both factors that lower caffeine. Matcha uses the youngest, most caffeine-dense leaf buds, consumed whole as a powder rather than strained out, the opposite profile on every count that affects caffeine.

## Flavor is a real factor too

Hojicha's roasted flavor reads as closer to coffee, toasty and low-bitterness. Matcha's flavor is grassy and vegetal, an acquired taste for some first-time drinkers that hojicha doesn't require.

## Which one to pick

If low caffeine is the priority, mushroom hojicha is the clear choice. If matcha's flavor and higher caffeine (with L-theanine's steadying effect) are what you're after, mushroom matcha is the closer match.
