## Confirmed: this is not a low-caffeine drink

Unlike some mushroom-blended teas built on roasted or lower-caffeine bases, mushroom matcha starts from matcha, one of the higher-caffeine forms of tea, because the whole leaf (young, shade-grown buds) is consumed as a powder rather than steeped and strained.

## The commonly cited range

Matcha's caffeine content is commonly cited in the 30-70mg per 8oz cup range, depending on how much powder is used, the specific leaf, and growing conditions. There is no single universal number; treat any source, including this one, that states one fixed figure for every cup with some skepticism.

| Drink | Commonly cited caffeine (per 8oz) |
|---|---|
| Coffee | ~80-100mg |
| Matcha | ~30-70mg |
| Sencha (standard green tea) | ~25-30mg |
| Mushroom hojicha | ~7-15mg |

## Why matcha is higher than most tea

Two factors: matcha uses young, shade-grown leaf buds (naturally higher in caffeine than mature leaf), and the whole leaf is consumed as a powder rather than strained out after steeping, so nothing is left behind.

## The honest positioning

Mushroom matcha's appeal isn't "less caffeine." It's the combination of caffeine with L-theanine, an amino acid naturally present in tea that's associated with steadier, less jittery focus, a different value proposition than a low-caffeine claim.
