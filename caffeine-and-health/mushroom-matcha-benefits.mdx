## From the tea

Matcha contains catechins, a class of antioxidant compound studied broadly in green tea research, along with L-theanine, an amino acid that appears to work alongside caffeine to support steadier, less jittery alertness than caffeine alone, an effect examined in several small human trials on tea and cognitive performance.

## From the mushrooms (traditional use, not medical claims)

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Lion's Mane">
    Traditionally used in East Asian cooking and herbal practice. A small number of human studies have examined Lion's Mane and cognitive function in specific populations, with some showing modest improvement on standardized tests during supplementation. This is an active but limited research area, not a settled clinical finding.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Cordyceps">
    Traditionally used in Traditional Chinese Medicine for stamina and recovery. Some studies, largely in athletes or older adults, have examined exercise performance markers; results are mixed rather than uniformly positive, and many rely on small sample sizes.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Reishi">
    Traditionally used for relaxation and general wellness. Reishi contains triterpene compounds studied for their interaction with the immune system, primarily in animal and in-vitro research, with more limited human research. It is not a treatment for any medical condition.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What this page does not claim

No mushroom or tea compound discussed here treats, cures, or prevents any disease. Traditional-use and preliminary-research language is used deliberately, not as a softer way of stating a settled medical fact.
