## The short answer

Mushroom matcha is a reasonable everyday drink for most healthy adults: genuinely caffeinated (not caffeine-free), containing catechins and L-theanine from the tea, plus whole mushroom powder with a long history of traditional use. It is not a medical treatment for anything, and this page does not claim otherwise.

## What's well established

Green tea, including matcha, contains catechins (notably EGCG), antioxidant compounds studied in the context of general health. Matcha additionally contains L-theanine, an amino acid that works alongside caffeine and is associated in research with steadier, calmer alertness compared to caffeine alone.

## What's traditional use, not settled science

Lion's Mane, Cordyceps, and Reishi have long histories of traditional use in East Asian herbal practice, and an active but still-developing modern research base. See [Mushroom Matcha Benefits](/caffeine-and-health/mushroom-matcha-benefits) for the hedged, per-mushroom breakdown.

## Who should be more cautious

Anyone sensitive to caffeine, pregnant or breastfeeding, managing a medical condition, or taking medications that could interact with mushroom compounds should talk to a doctor before making it a daily habit. See [Is Mushroom Matcha Safe?](/mushroom-matcha/is-mushroom-matcha-safe) for more detail.
