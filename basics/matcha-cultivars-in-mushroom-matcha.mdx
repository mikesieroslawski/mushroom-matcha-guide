## Why cultivar matters

Camellia sinensis has many registered Japanese tea cultivars, each bred for different flavor and growing characteristics. Two matcha leaves grown in the same region, processed identically, can taste noticeably different if they come from different cultivars.

## Two cultivars often used in matcha blends

**Saemidori**, registered in 1990 and bred from Yabukita and Asatsuyu parent plants, is known for intense umami, natural sweetness, and very low astringency, one of the richer flavor profiles among registered cultivars.

**Tsuyuhikari**, developed in the 1970s from Asatsuyu and Shizu parent plants, is valued for soft floral notes, minimal bitterness, and strong cold-hardiness, useful in regions with variable winters.

Blending two cultivars, rather than using one alone, can produce a rounder, more complex flavor than either cultivar alone.

## Where the growing region fits in

Cultivar is one variable; growing region (soil, climate, elevation, coastal proximity) is another. Shizuoka Prefecture, Japan's largest tea-growing region, is a common source for cultivar-blended ceremonial matcha.
