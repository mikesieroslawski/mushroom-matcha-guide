## Step one: growing the tea

Ceremonial-grade matcha starts with shade-grown Camellia sinensis leaf. Shading the plants for several weeks before harvest increases chlorophyll and L-theanine content, part of what gives high-grade matcha its vivid green color and rounder, less bitter flavor than sun-grown tea leaf.

## Step two: processing into tencha

Harvested leaves are steamed to halt oxidation, dried flat without rolling, and de-stemmed and de-veined, producing tencha, the specific leaf form used only for matcha.

## Step three: stone-milling

Tencha is ground into matcha powder using traditional granite stone mills, a slow process (roughly 30-40 grams per hour per mill) that keeps friction-generated heat low, preserving aroma compounds and producing a finer, smoother powder than high-speed mechanical grinding.

## Step four: blending with mushroom powder

Separately processed, dried, whole mushroom powder (Lion's Mane, Cordyceps, Reishi) is blended into the finished matcha powder, the point at which the product becomes mushroom matcha rather than plain matcha.
