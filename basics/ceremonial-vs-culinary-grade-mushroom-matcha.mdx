## What "ceremonial grade" means

Ceremonial grade is the highest quality tier of matcha, made exclusively from the youngest first-harvest leaves, shade-grown, hand-sorted, and stone-milled, traditionally meant to be whisked with only water and consumed on its own. The term comes from its use in the Japanese tea ceremony.

## What "culinary grade" means

Culinary grade matcha is typically made from later-harvest or lower-tier leaf, processed with less selectivity. It's intended for baking, cooking, or blending into drinks with strong competing flavors (like a sweetened frappe), where matcha's subtler qualities matter less.

## Why this matters for a mushroom-blended product

A mushroom matcha built on ceremonial-grade tea gives the mushroom powder a genuinely high-quality flavor base to sit alongside. Built on culinary grade, the blend leans more on the mushroom powder and any added flavoring to carry the cup, since culinary-grade matcha alone is more bitter and less nuanced.

## How to tell which grade a product uses

A transparent label states the grade directly. Vague marketing language ("premium," "high-quality") without the word "ceremonial" specifically is a signal to look closer.
