## Why matcha is sensitive to storage

Matcha's fine grind and vivid color come from careful processing that's easy to undo with poor storage. Air exposure oxidizes matcha, dulling its green color toward brown-green and flattening its flavor. Light and heat accelerate the same degradation.

## How to store it

- **Keep it sealed.** Reseal the bag tightly after every use, pressing out excess air if possible.
- **Keep it cool.** A pantry away from the stove or direct sunlight is fine; refrigeration can help extend freshness further if the container is fully airtight (to avoid moisture and odor absorption).
- **Use it within a reasonable window.** Matcha is best within a few months of opening; the mushroom powder blended in doesn't meaningfully change this timeline.

## Signs it's past its best

A shift from vivid green toward dull olive or brown, and a flatter, less grassy flavor, are the two clearest signs matcha (mushroom-blended or not) has faded, even if it's technically still safe to drink.
